Chapter 2 · 植物研究 / Plant Research (130 words)

01photosynthesis/ˌfəʊtəʊˈsɪnθəsɪs/noun
Definition
The process by which green plants use sunlight to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose (sugar) and oxygen.
  • Photosynthesis takes place mainly in the leaves.
⚠ Native-speaker pitfallSix syllables — stress is on the third one: pho-to-SYN-the-sis. Many learners stress the first syllable, which makes it sound like a different word. And do not say “photosyntesis” — the th is real (/θ/).
📖 Oxford: photosynthesis
02respire/rɪˈspaɪə(r)/verb
Definition
1. (formal) to breathe. 2. (biology) for a plant or animal to take in oxygen from air or water and release carbon dioxide.
  • Fish respire through their gills.
⚠ Native-speaker pitfallThe verb respire is technical / formal; in everyday English we just say breathe. In IELTS writing about plant biology, “plants respire continuously” is sharper than “plants breathe all the time” — but never use respire for a person unless you are in a medical textbook.
📖 Oxford: respire
03botany/ˈbɒtəni/ BrE · /ˈbɑːtəni/ NAmEnoun
Definition
The scientific study of plants.
  • She has a degree in botany from Cambridge.
⚠ Native-speaker pitfallStress is on the first syllable: BOT-a-ny, not “bo-TA-ny”. And the adjective form is botanical (not “botanic”): “a botanical garden”, “botanical illustrations”.
📖 Oxford: botany
04ecology/iˈkɒlədʒi/ BrE · /iˈkɑːlədʒi/ NAmEnoun
Definition
1. the scientific study of the relationship between living things and their environment. 2. the pattern of relations between a particular group of organisms and their environment.
  • She is a professor of plant ecology.
⚠ Native-speaker pitfallPronounce the second syllable like “call”, not like “cold”: /i-KO-lə-dʒi/. And ecology ≠ economy. The o in the second syllable carries a lot of weight — slurring them together is the most common student mistake.
📖 Oxford: ecology
05ecosystem/ˈiːkəʊsɪstəm/ BrE · /ˈiːkoʊsɪstəm/ NAmEnoun
Definition
All the living things in an area and the way they affect each other and the environment.
  • The Amazon ecosystem is one of the most diverse on earth.
⚠ Native-speaker pitfallStress is on EE-co-sys-tem — three stresses almost equally, with the strongest on the first syllable. Don't put full stress on “-sys-”; that makes it sound like a different word. The plural is ecosystems.
📖 Oxford: ecosystem
06eco-friendly/ˌiːkəʊˈfrendli/adjective
Definition
Not harmful to the environment; designed to cause as little harm as possible to the environment.

⚠ Native-speaker pitfallAlways written with a hyphen: eco-friendly. And in formal writing prefer environmentally friendly or just sustainable — “eco-friendly” is a touch informal / marketing-ish.
📖 Oxford: eco-friendly
07vegetation/ˌvedʒəˈteɪʃn/noun
Definition

⚠ Native-speaker pitfallStress is on the third syllable: ve-ge-TA-tion. The noun is always singular and uncountable: “the vegetation of the region” — never “vegetations”. And don't say “vegetations” to mean “kinds of plants”; use plants or flora.
📖 Oxford: vegetation
08herb/hɜːb/ BrE · /ɜːrb/ NAmEnoun
Definition
1. a plant whose leaves, flowers or seeds are used to give flavour to food, or as medicine. 2. (also herb /hɜːb/) a soft, leafy plant without woody stems.

⚠ Native-speaker pitfallPronunciation is one of the classic BrE / NAmE splits: BrE /hɜːb/ (silent h); NAmE /ɜːrb/ (the h is pronounced). The r in NAmE is the giveaway. Don't write “erb” in BrE spelling.
